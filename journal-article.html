The Alchemy of Varanasi Katan: 500 Years of Warp & Weft

By Gayatri Devi • September 24, 2026 • 6 min read

Before the first gold thread pierces the mulberry warp, there is an unspoken dialogue between weaver and loom that has survived half a millennium of Indian dynastic shifts. In Varanasi, weaving is not an occupation; it is a spiritual invocation performed in rhythmic cadence.

“The loom does not distinguish between metal and silk; it demands patience, an unhurried pulse, and reverence for geometry.”

The Kadhwa technique, unique to Varanasi, allows our master artisans to engrave independent floral motifs (bootas) without any loose floats on the reverse side of the drape. It takes anywhere between 45 and 90 days to weave a single six-yard saree, passing through three generations of hands before it touches the shoulders of an AALAYA patron.

To touch an authentic Katan silk drape is to encounter the weight of time itself. The pure zari threads carry an antique champagne patina that resists oxidation, ensuring that what you wear today remains an untouched heirloom for your granddaughters.
